What a pool can and cannot do

It can never spend your coins. Delegating does not move your stake and does not put the pool's key anywhere near it. Your staking output keeps exactly one spending condition, your own key. What you lend sits in a separate little record naming you and the pool, and while that record is unspent the network counts your weight for the pool and expects the pool to sign the blocks.

You can leave whenever you like. Only your key can spend that record, so reclaiming needs nobody's cooperation and nobody's permission. There is no lock, no queue and no notice period on leaving. The weight is yours again the moment the reclaim confirms.

What you are trusting a pool for is the reward, and only the reward. Blocks pay their fees to whoever produced them. A pool that has committed to nothing on-chain keeps all of it, and that is the default rather than an abuse. Read the "Pays out" column before you delegate, and keep reading it afterwards.

A committed policy cannot change behind your back. Any change has to be announced on-chain a fixed number of blocks before it can bind, and every announcement in that window is shown at the top of this page and inside your own wallet. That waiting period is worth exactly as much as the attention you pay to it, which is why leaving is instant: you are meant to have time to read, decide and go.

The two committed modes differ in what they guarantee. A direct policy names an address that every block must pay, so the operator cannot silently redirect the reward, but the chain does not check that the address shares anything with you. A lottery policy makes each block pay one delegator drawn by stake weight from a seed rooted in Bitcoin's proof of work, so the draw cannot be rigged and your share over time is exactly proportional, at the cost of arriving in occasional lumps instead of steadily.

Reliability is the number nobody advertises. A pool holding a tenth of the network's stake should produce about a tenth of the blocks. Well under that means it is offline, missing its slots or losing races, and its delegators are earning nothing for the weight they lent it.

Joining a pool

From the desktop wallet, open the Staking tab, pick a pool in the "Staking pool" card and click Delegate. From a node, with the pool's signer key from the table above:

sequentia-cli delegatestake "<signer public key>"

Check what you are joining, and what it has announced, before and after:

sequentia-cli listpools          # this page, from your own node
sequentia-cli listdelegations    # where your stake signs, and any pending change

And to leave, at any moment, without asking anyone:

sequentia-cli undelegatestake

Delegating requires a registered stake. It does not require your wallet to be online afterwards, which is the other reason pools exist: the key that can move your coins never has to touch a block-producing server.
